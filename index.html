    About

    The Ethnic Bias Data Explorer is a project of the StopBias.org campaign to protect children from the harms of antiwhite bias.

    Around the world, ethnic European children (commonly called “White”) are being made vulnerable to physical and psychological harm by incomplete and distorted interracial crime data from governments, and by biased media coverage that inverts the reality of interracial crime rates.

    The Ethnic Bias Data Explorer shows what data we currently have, what it shows, where the gaps are, and where the biases lie in government statistics and in media reporting.

    The Ethnic Bias Data Explorer reveals the need for better data so that we can protect children from harm, make better decisions as individuals informed by accurate media, and have governments that provide and use accurate interracial crime data.

    The Crime tab scores ethnic data completeness from 0 to 100. Heritage is 75 points and is awarded only for a published cross-table that records child victim heritage and offender heritage for the same cases. One-sided data scores 0 for heritage. Coverage is 10 points, rates 7.5 and access 7.5. Neglect is not scored. The five harms are weighted homicide 27.78%, sexual abuse 22.22%, sex trafficking 22.22%, physical abuse 16.67% and online exploitation 11.11%. A proxy such as nationality, and a published “White” category that merges Hispanic people, cannot earn full heritage marks. A country with no real ethnicity data is capped at 25%.

    For more information about the StopBias.org campaign to protect ethnic European children from the harms of antiwhite bias, please visit our website and follow us on social media.

    What do we mean by ‘ethnic European’?

    Ethnic Europeans are people whose heritage traces to the native peoples of Europe, including those whose families settled in the Americas, Australia, New Zealand and elsewhere. They are commonly called ‘White’.

    ‘White’ describes skin colour, and many communities with light skin are not ethnic European. When we say antiwhite bias, we mean prejudice and discrimination aimed specifically at people of ethnic European heritage. This can more precisely be called ethnic Europhobia.

    Most official statistics don’t record ethnic European heritage at all, and use broad categories like ‘White’ that mix different groups together. This gap makes it hard to see when ethnic European children are being harmed, and it’s one of the gaps this project highlights.
